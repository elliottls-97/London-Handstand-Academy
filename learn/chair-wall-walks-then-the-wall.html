Being upside down is strange at first, and the wall behind you makes the way down unclear. So get used to it in an order where each step makes the next one safe.

1
Step 1 of 4

The chair assisted handstand.

Feet on a box or a chair, hands on the floor, and walk the hands in until your hips are over your shoulders and your shoulders are over your hands. It is a handstand shape with the feet held up for you. Upside down, stacked, no balance to worry about yet.

Hips over shoulders, shoulders over hands.
The green line is the whole drill.
If it bends, walk the hands in until it's straight.
  • Hands shoulder width, fingers spread
  • Shoulders directly over the hands
  • Hips directly over the shoulders
2
Step 2 of 4

Wall walks.

Start in a plank with your feet at the wall. Walk the feet up and the hands in, as far as feels fine, then walk back down. Push tall through the shoulders the whole way. Each time, a little further in.

Push tall the whole way.
  1. 1
    Plank, feet at the wallHands under the shoulders, body in one line.
  2. 2
    Feet up, hands inSmall steps. Push tall through the shoulders.
  3. 3
    As far as feels fineStop there. Nothing is gained by forcing the last bit.
  4. 4
    Walk back down with controlThe way down is part of the drill.
Build it

Each time, a little further in. The hands finish closer to the wall over weeks, not in one session.

3
Step 3 of 4

Learn to fall.

Before you go all the way in, learn the way out. Walk up with the hands far from the wall, take one leg out to the side to bring that hip down, step a hand and turn.

One leg out, and down.

4
Step 4 of 4

Chest to wall handstand.

Now all the way in. Hold for twenty to thirty seconds and come down the way you practised.

Stacked, reaching tall.
  • Chest and toes at the wall
  • Legs squeezed together
  • Reaching tall through the toes
  • Ribs gently in
  • Hold 20 to 30 seconds, then bail the way you practised
One cue, for all of it

Push the floor away.

Shoulders up by the ears, arms straight, the whole body reaching upwards rather than sitting in the joints. It is the cue under every drill on this page and under the press at the top of the ladder.

Shoulders up by the ears.
