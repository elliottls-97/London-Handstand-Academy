Put your hands on top before you pull.

The false grip puts the wrist over the bar before the pull starts, so at the top there is far less to turn over. Five steps, from setting it to pulling in it.

In an ordinary grip the hand hangs under the bar. At the top of a muscle up it has to end up on top of it, and turning it over in the middle of the move is where a lot of attempts stall.

The false grip starts with the heel of the hand already on top and the wrist bent over the bar or ring. There is much less left to turn, so the transition from under the bar to over it gets shorter.

It feels strange on the wrists and forearms at first, and it is easy to lose as soon as you pull. So it is learned in order: set it, hang in it with help, then pull in it. Little and often, never all at once.

1
Step 1 of 5Standing

Set it on the bar.

Stand under the bar and set one hand at a time before you hang from anything. The heel of the hand goes on top of the bar, and the wrist bends over it, so the bar sits in the crease where the wrist meets the hand.

It will feel odd, and the forearms will notice. That is the grip, not a mistake.

  1. 1
    Heel of the hand on top of the barNot hanging underneath it.
  2. 2
    Wrist bent over the barThe bar sits in the crease.
  3. 3
    Hold it as you hangOnly as long as the wrist stays bent.
Build it

Set each hand standing, then hold it as you take weight. If the wrist straightens, that set is over.

2
Step 2 of 5Supported

Hang, with the feet helping.

Next, hang in the grip with your feet on a box or the floor. The feet take whatever the hands cannot yet, so you can practise the position without the whole of your weight on your wrists.

As it gets easier, put less into the feet. Short holds are plenty to begin with.

  1. 1
    Wrists stay bent over the barThe moment they open, stand up.
  2. 2
    Feet take what the hands can'tLess in the feet as it gets easier.
Build it

Short holds, feet helping. Take weight off the feet a little at a time, never all at once.

3
Step 3 of 5Rings

Set it on the rings.

On the rings the idea is the same, and the rings let the hand settle. The ring goes deep in the crease of the wrist, the palm comes over the top, and the thumb wraps round.

  1. 1
    Ring in the crease of the wristDeep in, not in the fingers.
  2. 2
    Palm over the top, thumb aroundThe hand sits on top of the ring.
  3. 3
    Wrist stays bent as you hangSame rule as the bar.
Build it

Set one ring at a time, then the other, before you lift your feet at all.

4
Step 4 of 5Supported

Hang on the rings, feet on the box.

Set the grip standing up, then sit back with your feet staying down on the box. You are hanging in the false grip with your legs taking part of the load.

  1. 1
    Set the grip standing upBoth hands before you sit back.
  2. 2
    Sit back, feet stay downThe box carries the rest.
  3. 3
    Wrists stay bent over the ringsIf they open, reset.
Build it

Set, sit back, hold. As it settles, let the feet take less.

5
Step 5 of 5On the bar

Pull, and keep it.

Once the hang is comfortable, pull in the grip. Chest to the bar, and the wrists stay over it the whole way up and down. This is what a muscle up asks of the grip, and it is the step that takes longest.

  1. 1
    Keep the false gripSet it before the first rep.
  2. 2
    Pull the chest to the barNot just the chin over it.
  3. 3
    Wrists stay overAll the way up and all the way down.
Build it

A few clean reps with the grip held, rather than more reps where it slips.

Before you add more

Look after your hands.

The false grip rubs the skin on the little finger side of the wrist, which is not as tough as your palm. Too much too soon is how it tears, so start with short holds a couple of times a week and add a little at a time.

Chalk helps the grip stay put. Keep calluses filed flat, so they do not catch and rip.

Some tiredness in the forearms is normal. If something hurts more each session rather than less, rest until the next one, and if it does not settle, have a physio look at it before you load it again.
