Learn to come down before you try to stay up.

Three ways out of a handstand, built from low kicks until they happen on their own. Fear of the crash is what keeps beginners on the wall for a year. This is how to take it away.

I didn't have much fear of falling when I came to handstands. A lot of my students have, and I've watched it keep people leaning on the wall for a year. So this is the thing I spend the most time on with beginners, and it's the thing that unlocks everything after it.

Here's the problem. If part of you is braced for a crash, you won't balance. You'll kick up half heartedly, you'll bail early, and you'll never spend enough time upside down to learn anything. Balance only starts once your body knows, without thinking, that it can get out of any position.

So learn the way down first. Three ways.

1
Way 1 of 3Start here

Jump and turn.

The basic bail out. Hands down, a small kick, nowhere near vertical, turn your hips and land on your feet next to your hands. That's it. By the time your kick is big enough to actually go over, the turn is already there.

Clip to film
The jump and turn, side on
Small kick, hips turn, feet land beside the hands. Low first, then higher. Same gym as the bail outs so it matches.
  1. 1
    Hands down, a small kickNowhere near vertical. Back leg to hip height is plenty.
  2. 2
    Turn your hipsBoth feet leave the floor and swing round to the side.
  3. 3
    Land next to your handsFeet down beside where your hands are.
  4. 4
    Stand up. Go again.That's it. Repeat until it's boring.
Build it

Do it low until it's boring, then a bit higher each time. The height follows the confidence, never the other way round.

2
Way 2 of 3 · The one you keep

The cartwheel out.

Kick up, get your hips over your hands, and when it goes too far, fall out to the side. Not over the top. One hand steps, you turn, your feet land one after the other and you're standing up.

Build it the same way as the jump and turn, from low kicks. The difference is the hips travel over the hands before you come down.

The forward roll
What gymnastics teaches first
  • Works in a gym with mats
  • Painful on a hard floor
  • Takes effort, so you won't want to kick straight back up
The cartwheel out
What we teach instead
  • Works on any floor
  • You land on your feet, standing
  • Costs nothing, so you go straight back up. That's the whole point

Suryava came through gymnastics, where they teach the forward roll out first. If every bail is a roll, you won't want to kick straight back up. Out of a cartwheel you can.

3
Way 3 of 3 · For the wall

The chest to wall bail out.

Chest to wall is where people get properly scared, because the wall is behind you and you can't see the way down. This is the way down. It's the thing that got my more nervous students through that stage.

One leg out, and down.
  1. 1
    Walk up with your hands a long way from the wallFurther out than 45 degrees to begin with.
  2. 2
    Take one leg out to the sideUse it to bring that hip down towards the floor.
  3. 3
    Step one hand out and twistOut to the side, away from the wall.
  4. 4
    Back on your feetFacing away from the wall, standing.
Build it

Walk the hands in. As it gets comfortable, start a bit closer to the wall each time, until you can bail from a full chest to wall handstand.

The point of all three

Until you stop thinking about it.

Being able to do these isn't the goal. Doing them without a thought is.

Practise each one until your body answers on its own when a handstand goes wrong. Then the conscious part of you and the subconscious part both know it's safe, and something changes: you stop thinking about falling and start thinking about holding. That's when you can really start learning to balance.
