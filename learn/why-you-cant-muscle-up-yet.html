Why you can't muscle up yet.

Five things that stop a muscle up, and the fix for each. Most of them have nothing to do with how many pull ups you can do.

It's really common to be able to do ten strict pull ups and still not get a muscle up. It feels like it should come with the strength, and it doesn't.

A pull up gets your chin over the bar. A muscle up needs your chest over the bar, and the only thing that gets it there is speed. A slow pull runs out of height right where the muscle up starts.

So the fix isn't more pull ups. It's pulling differently: faster, higher and on a slightly different line. Then you need a dip that's strong at the bottom, because that's where you land.

1
Step 1 of 5Start here

Pull faster.

Most of us train pull ups slowly and with control, which is right for strength. But a muscle up is a power move: enough force, moved quickly. If you've never trained pulling fast, you won't have it when you need it.

Start every rep from a proper dead hang, and pull as hard and as fast as you can. A band under your feet takes some of the weight so you can move quicker than you could on your own. Then take the band away.

Fast, from a dead hang.
  1. 1
    Dead hang firstArms long, a still start, every rep.
  2. 2
    Pull hard and fastMake it explosive, not smooth.
  3. 3
    Aim low on the bodyThe bar heads for your waist.
  4. 4
    Stop when it slowsA slow rep teaches slow.

In the Muscle Up Ladder: Swinging C Pulls and Swinging High Pulls, stage 2.

Build it

Fast reps only. When the speed goes, the set's over.

2
Step 2 of 5

Pull higher.

Chin over the bar is the finish line for a pull up, and it's about halfway for a muscle up. You need the bar down at your lower chest, ideally lower, before there's any room to get over it.

Chest, not chin.
  1. 1
    Think chest, not chinThe bar comes to the bottom of your ribs.
  2. 2
    Drive the elbows down and backBehind you, not out to the sides.
  3. 3
    Fast upUse the speed from step 1.
  4. 4
    Slow downLower with control, then a full hang.

In the Muscle Up Ladder: High C Pull Ups, stage 2. The check point is fifteen high pull ups in three goes or fewer.

Build it

Every rep, the bar touches the same low spot on your chest. If it doesn't, the set's over.

3
Step 3 of 5

Pull back a little.

On rings you go straight up between them. A bar is in the way, so you have to go round it. If you pull straight up, your face meets the bar. Pull up and slightly back, in a C shape, and you clear it with room to come over the top.

It starts before you pull. Hang with your arms fully overhead and your chest just behind the bar, then drive along that same angle.

Up, back, then over.
  1. 1
    Set the angle in the hangChest slightly behind the bar.
  2. 2
    Lean back as you pullA small amount, not a lot.
  3. 3
    Bar to the lower chestThe same low spot as step 2.
  4. 4
    Then forward and overThe turnover comes from there.

In the Muscle Up Ladder: High C Pull Ups, and Rings Levered Pull Ups for the same line on rings.

Build it

Up and back, then over. If you hit the bar, pull back a little more.

4
Step 4 of 5

Dip deeper.

The muscle up finishes in the bottom of a dip, chest over the bar. If your dips are shallow, you've never been strong down there, and the turnover dies even after a good pull.

The two halves meet in the middle. The higher you can pull and the deeper you can dip, the smaller the gap between them, and that gap is the transition.

Deep, and strong.
  1. 1
    Lower slowlyAs deep as you can control.
  2. 2
    Elbows tight and backNot flaring out to the sides.
  3. 3
    Chest over the barBreastbone low.
  4. 4
    Hold the bottomThen press back up.

In the Muscle Up Ladder: Straight Bar Dips and Ring Dips, stage 1. Low Dip Hold, on the bar and on rings, stage 3.

Build it

Pull high, dip deep, and the bit in between gets smaller.

5
Step 5 of 5

Train it fresh.

Speed is the first thing to go when you're tired. Grinding out sets of slow pulls just trains you to be slow. Keep the sets short and stop as soon as the height drops. Then rest properly, a few minutes, so the next set is just as fast.

  1. 1
    A few repsAround five to eight, not to failure.
  2. 2
    Stop when it dropsThe moment the height goes.
  3. 3
    Rest properlyA few minutes between sets.
  4. 4
    First in the sessionDo it while you're fresh.
Build it

Quality over quantity. Fewer, faster reps beat more slow ones.

Before you start

Muscle ups put a lot through your elbows, shoulders and wrists, and a false grip, for rings, takes time to get used to. Build up gradually, and stop if anything hurts. If it does, get it looked at by a physio: we work with Beth Shum, a Chartered Physiotherapist.
